# Week 3 – Linux Practice, Mini-Server Project & Back to the Basics [12.10 – 18.10.26]

This week didn't go exactly as planned. After working through the four foundations of Phase 1, I thought I was ready to move on to Phase 2 of Nana's roadmap. But I realized that I had overestimated my understanding of some topics, especially Linux.

I had learned a lot of commands individually, but knowing what a command does and actually knowing how to use it are two different things. When I tried to combine commands or solve problems on my own, I noticed that I was still missing some of the basics.

At the same time, I continued practicing Linux and worked on my mini-server project in GitHub Codespaces. I set up Nginx, created a backup script, configured a cron job, and worked with users and file permissions. This helped me connect different Linux concepts instead of just learning them separately.

I also started practicing troubleshooting scenarios. For example, when a website is unavailable, I learned to check the service status, look at the logs, test the response with `curl`, and start the service if necessary. I also worked through a scenario involving a full disk, using `df -h` and `du -sh` to find out where the space was being used before deciding what could safely be cleaned up.

Even though I covered quite a few topics, I noticed that some things still need more practice. I struggle most when I have to combine commands and figure out the right steps without looking at my notes. I also need to revisit Linux paths, permissions, package management, and cron troubleshooting.

Because of this, I decided not to rush into Phase 2 just to stay on schedule. Instead, I'm going back to the basics and changing how I learn. So far, I've spent a lot of time reading notes and memorizing commands. Going forward, I want to spend more time in the terminal, experimenting, making mistakes, and figuring things out for myself.

This week reminded me that progress isn't just about learning new things or moving through a roadmap as quickly as possible. Sometimes it's about taking a step back and making sure you actually understand what you've already learned.
